It is the count of odds within 10 points, but nothing says so, so the player sees a mystery number before the first action. It counts as chrome noise on a phone.

## science:royal (7 issues)
- **[high] v2/royal.js RY_TEXT.take (l.110), levelTake[2] (l.128), whatifBody.nomut (l.107), teacher.prompts[1] (l.217); docs/changes/wave2/royal.md instructor note ('selection on males alone is slow')**: The L3 lesson gets the standard result backwards. A recessive X-linked allele is exposed in every hemizygous male, so selection removes it much faster than an autosomal recessive. When rare, Δq ≈ −s·q/3, which is linear in q, so q falls geometrically; an autosomal recessive falls only as −s·q², which is hyperbolic. At balance the gap is large: q_m = 3μ/s on the X against √(μ/s) on an autosome (μ=1e-5, s=0.5: 6e-5 against 4.5e-3; Haldane 1935; Hartl & Clark ch. on X-linkage). The game's own model contradicts the text. In the 'No new mutations' What if?, the deterministic recursion from q≈0.33 with s=0.5 leaves q_m ≈ 1.6e-6 after 60 generations (s=1: 6e-19). The card would therefore read '0.0% of males had white eyes, against 5.9% with mutation. Selection on males alone removes white slowly.' Students are taught that the X lets bad recessives linger, which is the opposite of the textbook point and of the faster-X idea.
  - Fix: Reframe it as 'exposed in males, so purged quickly, and held at a low balance by mutation'. Compare with an autosomal recessive at the same s and μ: on the X it falls far faster and settles far lower, 3μ/s against √(μ/s). If the 'two of three copies sit in females' point stays, compare it only with selection on both sexes (or on a dominant allele), and say so. Rewrite take, levelTake[2], whatifBody.nomut ('…fell to {a}%: with every carrier son exposed, selection clears white within decades of generations; only mutation holds it at {b}%'), teacher prompt 2 and the log's instructor note. Consider an autosomal-recessive control series in the nomut What if?. Log the change in royal.md.
- **[medium] v2/royal.js MODEL level 2 'cannot' (l.151)**: '…or mosaic carrier females' is wrong for Drosophila. Flies do not inactivate an X: dosage compensation works by hyper-transcribing the single male X (MSL complex). So w/+ females are not mosaics, and the model is not leaving out a real fly phenomenon. Mosaicism by X inactivation is a mammalian feature, correctly mentioned only in the boss MODEL.
  - Fix: Drop 'mosaic carrier females' from the L3 MODEL. If needed, replace it with a real omission, such as 'white-eyed females' own mating and viability costs' or 'the visual basis of the mating deficit varying with light'.
- **[medium] v2/royal.js cvSpec line label 'exact' (l.~1141, D.lines.exact) and boss 'cv' ask expectation 0.0064 (l.411)**: The card draws q² = 0.64% labelled 'exact' as the answer for 'the share of women', but the real figure is about 0.4% (Birch 2012). The game's own why.real says so: protan and deutan variants sit at two genes, so the expected rate is q_p² + q_d² ≈ 0.02² + 0.06² ≈ 0.4%. Calling the single-locus Hardy–Weinberg number 'exact' presents a known-wrong prediction as the truth.
  - Fix: Relabel the line 'q² (one gene)' or 'prediction', and draw or mention the observed 0.4% beside it. Optionally centre the band so both are in it; the current band (0.34–0.94%) already contains 0.4%.
- **[low] v2/royal.js RY_TEXT.interesting.high (l.83)**: The text says 'It removes half of them each generation', but balAsk sets the lamp's share to 0.5, 0.25 or 1 (l.385), and the HUD names that share. On runs with a quarter or all, this result text misstates the selection coefficient the player was scored against.
  - Fix: Fill the share from s.sAuto through the same fH/fQ/fA words (e.g. 'It removes {f} white males each generation'), or word it without a number.
- **[low] v2/royal.js boss plaque 'exact {p}%' / MODEL L3 simulated; RY_CASES voh**: The 'exact' posteriors condition only on each woman's sons and leave out known pedigree evidence. Example: Victoria of Hesse's daughter Alice of Battenberg had an unaffected son (Prince Philip). Including that evidence gives P(carrier) = (1/4·3/4)/(1/4·3/4+3/4) ≈ 16%, against the 20% shown. The MODEL text is transparent ('from her parents, then from her sons'), but the plaque and the card call the number exact without qualification.
  - Fix: Say 'exact, from her parents and sons' on the plaque or in the MODEL simplification, e.g. add 'Daughters' and grandsons' evidence is left out.' The scoring needs no change: 16% is within 10 points.
- **[low] v2/royal.js levelWhy[0].real (l.168)**: 'These crosses tied a gene to a particular chromosome for the first time' overstates Morgan 1910. That paper explained the result with 'sex-limited' factors and did not name the X chromosome. Morgan made the chromosomal link in 1911, and Bridges' non-disjunction work (1916) is generally taken as the proof.
  - Fix: Reword to something like 'These crosses led Morgan, by 1911, to place a gene on a particular chromosome, the X, for the first time; Bridges proved it in 1916.' Add bridges1916 to refs if used.
- **[low] v2/royal.js whatifBody.auto (l.105) with bodyVars n===1 whatif (a = (2qf+qm)/3)**: 'Both sexes reached {a}% white in one generation' fills in 50, which is the white allele's share of copies. Only about 25% of flies are white-eyed. Elsewhere in the unit 'white' alone tends to read as eye colour, so students may take this as 50% white-eyed flies.
  - Fix: Say '{a}% white X copies' or 'a white allele share of {a}%', matching the L2 chart's 'White X share' wording.
