Files: `games/endless-forms/source/ch14.js` (The Pathogen), `ch20.js` (The Ring, Doubling), `ch21.js` (Two Fruits), `ch23.js` (The Guest), `ch26.js` (The Sweep, The Clock), `ch27.js` (Copy and Paste, The Archipelago), `ch28.js` (Tree Thinking, The Food Web), `ch29.js` (Islands, After the Fire).

Sources: `docs/reviews/R3` and its fact-check `R7_factcheck_R3`, which wins where the two disagree, plus `R8` on misconceptions. I re-checked every claim myself and read every line of player-facing text and model code in these files. I re-ran the Pathogen (optima, best reply, ESS) and Two Fruits models in Node with the game's own equations; the numbers below come from those runs. I checked the greenish warbler song pattern against the Irwin lab's summary of Irwin (2000) and Irwin, Bensch & Price (2001).

Almost all edits are to text. There are two code changes: the Ring song-card generator (the old one drew the wrong gradient) and the Clock's pairing of names with dates (the old one paired them at random). Neither changes scoring or balance.

## Considered but NOT changed

- **R3: "make myxoma the Pathogen's anchor"; "say the ring species are messier"; "add the Mexican origin of the *Rhagoletis* inversions".** All three are already in the `real` text (R7 PA4, RG2, FR4). Rejected.
- **R7 FR3: "host fidelity is not a magic trait".** The game never uses the term "magic trait". It says one trait does "two jobs — choosing a habitat and choosing a mate", which is correct for host preference with mating on the host. No change.
- **Two Fruits: the race index measures linkage disequilibrium, not reproductive isolation.** That is true, but `maths` defines it openly as a correlation and the categories are labelled as the game's own thresholds. My Node run reproduces every result the text claims: swarm 0.23 (one population); fruit mating 0.71 (two races); fruits ripening almost together 0.13; unlinked at two weeks 0.53 (partial); inversion 1.00; perfect fidelity 1.00. No change.
- **Pathogen: "a dead host infects no one".** Some pathogens do transmit from cadavers (anthrax, baculoviruses; Walther & Ewald's "sit-and-wait" pathogens). It is a fair framing for myxoma and for the model. Kept.
- **Pathogen model: the vector switch only removes the mobility penalty.** Kept. It agrees with myxoma (a vector does not remove the cost of host death), and the new text says so.
- **Ewald and the RECALL card.** `ch99.js` `RECALL.virulence` still presents Ewald's argument as the settled answer ("When transmission does not need a mobile host…"), and `RECALL.ring` says neighbours "interbreed all the way round" without the hedge. **Outside my files**, so not changed. The owner of `ch99.js` should hedge both (R7 M4, M20).
- **Food Web: "The sea star was rare".** Whether *Pisaster* is rare by biomass is arguable. The keystone definition (large effect relative to abundance; Power et al. 1996) still fits, and the invented abundance numbers are now flagged as illustrative. Kept.
- **Archipelago: "survivors' offspring had beaks about 4% deeper".** This agrees with Boag & Grant 1981 and later Grant & Grant summaries (roughly a 4–5% response). Kept.
- **Clock L2: calibration noise.** A 25 Myr calibration gives only about 48 differences, so "best estimates" can sit 20–25% from the simulated date (in one run, 391 for a 312 Myr split). This is pre-existing design, and the L1 card explains the scatter. Not changed; flagged for the redesign.
- **Clock: human–chimpanzee at 7 Myr.** TimeTree's point estimate is about 6.4. Estimates span roughly 6–8 Myr (fossil *Sahelanthropus* about 7 Myr; Langergraber et al. 2012). Kept at 7; the code comment records the range.
- **Tree: whales coded as lacking hair.** Adult whales are almost hairless (some keep bristles). A secondary loss is a legitimate character state for this exercise. Kept.
- **Doubling: `VIG = 1.8` vigour advantage.** Many neopolyploids are not more vigorous at all. I kept it because it makes level 1 winnable, and the text now discloses it as "a generous boost" (and `ch99.js` `MODEL.double` already says so).

- **Fire L2 Q3: "An oak suppresses a shrub strongly".** This is a Lotka–Volterra example, not a claim about fire. Kept.

## Needs a gameplay redesign to fix properly

- **The Pathogen** still has the player set the trait (R8 misconception 1). Text now frames it as choosing a strain, but the fix is strains competing across the warren, with heritable replication rate, and a co-infection level where the ESS (not a best reply to a fixed rival) emerges. Imperfect vaccines (Marek's disease; Read et al. 2015) would be a strong third act.
- **The Archipelago L1** has the player set the beak. Let the player set the seed supply or the competitor, and let beaks evolve. L2 is still a quiz.
- **The Ring** model is a clean 12-site ring with a single break. Real rings have internal breaks and historical gaps (Pereira & Wake 2009; Alcaide et al. 2014). A level where a hidden internal break must also be found would teach this better than a caveat.
- **Doubling**: the "Double the chromosomes" button implies somatic doubling. A meiosis minigame that produces unreduced gametes, plus the triploid bridge, would show the usual real routes. Level 1's 1.8× vigour boost props up the gameplay.
- **Two Fruits** is all prediction quiz. Selection acts on timing, not on preference.
- **The Guest** lacks partner choice versus sanctions and mutualism breakdown; L2 is a quiz.
- **The Sweep** needs decoys (a bottleneck, background selection) with a false-positive penalty, and SFS / Tajima's D. Text can only warn.
- **The Clock** runs every lineage at one rate. A fast-clock rodent pair (relative rate test), generation time, calibration uncertainty and a relaxed clock all need play, not text. A bigger calibration would cut the noise noted above.
- **Copy and Paste**: the TE share of genomes (about 45% of the human genome), TE domestication (RAG1/2, syncytin), whole-genome duplication, and the pseudogenisation rate in play.
- **Tree Thinking**: removing "lives in water" also removed the only aquatic "trap". A deliberate convergence trap (one that supports a *wrong* pairing) would teach homoplasy better. Rooting, outgroups and long-branch attraction are absent.
- **The Food Web**: the removal data other than *Pisaster* are invented. A living tidepool, or real removal data (for example Paine 1974; Menge et al. 1994), would fix that.
- **Islands**: SLOSS (single large or several small) and reserve design are absent.
- **After the Fire**: a three-stage replacement chain guarantees a diversity hump, so the game rewards the IDH as a rule however the text hedges. Landscapes with monotonic or flat diversity–disturbance relationships would fix this.
