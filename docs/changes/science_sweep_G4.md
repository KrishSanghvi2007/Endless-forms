## Considered but NOT changed

- **River maths, "about four or five epochs on average"** (ch6.js:12). Correct: a 200,000-run simulation gives a mean of 4.76 epochs to reach 3 clashes. 0.55³ ≈ 17% is correct, and k² pairs is correct (R7-R3 RV2).
- **River, "Dobzhansky (1936)".** Many sources cite his 1937 book. But his 1936 *Genetics* paper on *D. pseudoobscura* hybrid sterility already argued for interacting factors. Not clearly wrong, so I left it and added Bateson.
- **River level 2's hybrid survival of 0.2** stays as a model assumption. The chorus-frog citation (Lemmon 2009) is about call divergence, which is right. R7-R3 also confirmed Coyne & Orr 1989 and Lemmon 2009.
- **Lek optima** (ch6.js:137). "About 2, 4 and 7" is right: computed 2.10, 3.75, 6.55. "Settles faster in fog" is also right: less reliable cues give less value from searching. I checked the backward-induction code (`values`, `accept1`) and it is consistent.
- **Lek, "yellow air sacs"; golden eagles on leks.** Both fine.
- **Meadow level 2 mechanism** (pollination only when tongue < spur, Φ((L − μ)/2)). Correct (Darwin; Nilsson 1988). R2's point that the best spur is a constant offset (+4.5 cm) is true but needs a redesign (see below). "Forty years before anyone found it" (1862 → 1903) is fine.
- **Meadow, "dilute nectar" in hummingbird flowers.** Right as a tendency.
- **Live Fast, Williams (1966) on extrinsic mortality and early reproduction.** Reasonable attribution: residual reproductive value. Abrams' (1993) caveat concerns the evolution of senescence. I left it out of the text to save length; the new "ageing is fixed here" clause covers the model's limits.
- **Live Fast, R2 "Among foxes the optimum is 100% at every age."** Confirmed (DP: 100% at ages 1–8; the island runs 60 → 100%). The end text is accurate. That this makes the level trivial is a design problem.
- **Live Fast, "Y-model trade-off" as a name for the cost of reproduction.** Loose but acceptable. I added the acquisition/allocation point instead of renaming it.
- **Shape-shifters maths** (threshold 0.15/0.65 ≈ 23%; √32 ≈ 5.7). Correct. The three ponds' best policies (follow, ignore → no helmet, always helmet) match the level 1 note.
- **Crowd, Milinski's 2:1 ratio and "within minutes".** Correct as I recall the 1979 paper (six fish, 2:1, then reversed). The cascade logic matches Bikhchandani et al. 1992 with own-signal tie-breaking (R2 agrees).
- **Commons level 1 numbers.** Correct: 49% → 54% overall; producers fall in every jar; the rich jar grows 3.1× the poor jar. **Commons hook, "Inside any group, cheaters always do better".** True of this model. The `real` text already gives the invertase exception (Gore et al. 2009).
- **Lock and Key, Dybdahl & Lively 1998 and Decaestecker 2007.** Correct (R7-R3).
- **Lock and Key, "tetrodotoxin in amounts that could kill many people".** "Several" might be more exact, but it is not wrong. Left.

- **Many Become One, settling ∝ N^(2/3).** Correct Stokes scaling (R2). The upward mutation bias in cluster size (65% up) adds about +0.14 cells a day without selection. That is small against the selection response, so I left it.

- **R3, "no organisms" in Lock and Key.** Rejected by R7-R3 LK7. Not a science issue anyway.

## Needs a gameplay redesign to fix properly

- **Players choose optima; selection is never shown** (Lek, Live Fast, Shape-shifters, Crowd, Commons, Lock level 2). Text can only state the adaptationist step. A proper fix would make the player's strategy an allele competing in a visible population (R2 cross-cutting point 1).
- **River level 2:** call divergence (`d = 3 × overlap`) is scripted and independent of the player's choices. Female preference is not modelled. Reinforcement should emerge from heritable preference and call variation.
- **Crowd levels 1–2:** the crowd is scripted to drift to the ideal free distribution, so undermatching, interference and travel costs cannot appear. The new `real` text describes them but the model cannot show them.
- **Commons level 2:** there is no way to separate weak from strong altruism (a self-benefit dial), and the threshold b/k > c is printed in Notes.
- **Live Fast:** level 1 among foxes is a corner solution, and ageing is imposed rather than evolved. A senescence level is needed to make the Austad citation playable.
- **Meadow level 2:** the best spur is always mean tongue + about 4.5 cm. Orchid and moth should be two coevolving distributions. The subtitle "Pollination and fertilisation" (in `ui.js`, not my group) promises fertilisation content that does not exist.
- **Lek:** the subtitle "Mate choice and runaway" (in `ui.js`, not my group) promises a runaway that is never modelled. There is also no lek paradox and no copying, and level 1's sequential no-recall search is not how real hens choose.
- **Shape-shifters level 2:** the twenty-season geometric-mean lineage is computed analytically and never shown.
- **Many Become One level 2:** a cheater rate of 6% per division is needed to make the whack-a-mole playable, and the single-cell bottleneck is stated, not played.
- **Lock and Key level 1:** the player is a forecaster, not a parasite lineage, and the link to Why Sex? is only a sentence.
