# R4: Equilibrium (Hardy–Weinberg) and Tangled Bank: critical review

*Raw reviewer output (senior evolutionary biologist + commercial game designer). Not edited for tone. Line references are to `games/equilibrium-hw/source/` and `games/tangled-bank/source/`.*

## Verdict in one paragraph

Equilibrium is the most carefully scaffolded thing in the repository and the least like a game. Each level is a guided worksheet: read a panel, answer 1–4 multiple-choice questions or drag a slider, watch a short deterministic animation, get stars for being right. The player almost never makes a decision whose consequence they have to live with. Its best asset is the de Finetti triangle used as a map ("sideways = evolution, off the curve = genotype packaging"). The model code is mostly correct (χ² with 1 df, Δq under complete selection, 2pq/q² carrier ratios), but I found three real errors and several misleading generalisations, listed below. Tangled Bank is the opposite: it is visually the richest of the three games (textured bark, leaf litter, streams, a growing title tree) and has a real individual-based diploid engine (`core.js` `Gen`), but its Steward chapters are mostly "flip one toggle, then wait". The combined game should keep Equilibrium's pedagogy, Tangled Bank's look and engine, and Endless Forms' breadth, and should stop running three genetics engines, three visual languages and two separate Hardy–Weinberg courses (Equilibrium and Endless Forms' *The Census*).

## Confirmed errors (fix before anything else)

1. **Equilibrium Island migration text is backwards.** `islandStep` sets `p = 0.85p + 0.15 × 0.1` (lv_b.js:104), so migrants carry mostly **a** (p = 0.1). Over 12 generations p falls from 0.60 to 0.17 and the dot moves right, toward "all aa". The explanation says "Migrants with **few a alleles** pulled the dot…" (lv_b.js:126). A student who reads the map correctly is told they are wrong.
2. **"Non-random mating never changes allele frequencies" is false for disassortative mating.** The l14 summary says "Non-random mating changes genotype frequencies without changing allele frequencies… mating with opposites makes them commoner" and the quiz says "Non-random mating repackages alleles without changing how many there are" (lv_d.js:18). The game hides the exception by always starting at p = 0.5. Run the game's own `matingDist(f,'dis')` from p = 0.8 and p drops to 0.70 in one generation, then settles at 0.71. Disassortative mating is negative frequency-dependent sexual selection: the rare type gets more matings. That is exactly why self-incompatibility alleles in plants and MHC diversity are maintained (Wright 1939). The true statement is narrower: inbreeding, and *complete* assortative mating where every type is equally fertile, change genotype frequencies but not allele frequencies.
3. **The bird can see genotypes.** In l9 the panel says "Try the genotype lens: the bird can't" (lv_c.js:12), but `tap()` (lv_c.js:14) ignores the lens. Players eat heterozygotes through the genotype lens and beat the q ≤ 0.30 target by cheating, which wipes out the level's lesson ("selection only sees phenotype"). Disable eating while the genotype lens is on, or make the lens cost time.
4. **Minor data inconsistencies.** Case "The vanishing yellow" (lv_d.js:87) gives 70% vs 40% survival (s = 0.43 against aa). From q = 0.5 that predicts p = 0.56, 0.61, 0.65 … 0.80, but the trace shows 0.53, 0.56 … 0.74. Case "Like with like" (lv_d.js:90) is a symmetric trajectory, but colour-based assortment under complete dominance (the game's own `phenAssort`) is asymmetric: [0.25, 0.5, 0.25] goes to [0.33, 0.33, 0.33] in one step. Equilibrium Island's mating switch uses *genotypic* assortment (`matingDist 'assort'`, which is mathematically identical to selfing), while l14 uses *phenotypic* assortment. So the same label hides two different models.
5. **Tangled Bank, Black Country:** carbonaria has a recurrent mutation rate of `mu: 0.01` per gamete (hunt.js:332). The chapter's own "real" text (hunt.js:330) correctly says carbonaria arose once, as a transposon insertion in *cortex* dated to about 1819 (van't Hof et al. 2016). A 1% forward rate in a population of 30 creates new black moths every generation. Set mu to 0, or allow one event.
6. **Tangled Bank, Aripo:** "choosy females end up carrying the genes of the colourful males they pick" (steward.js:218). It is their *offspring* that carry both the preference and the colour alleles. That linkage disequilibrium between preference and colour is the Fisherian mechanism, and the sentence should name it.

## Equilibrium, level by level

**L1 Two copies each.** *Does:* toggles two wing cases, flips a lens, then classifies 8 beetles (4 buttons each); about 2 min. *Science:* fine. It never says the beetle is diploid or that this is an autosomal locus, and these assumptions matter later (X-linkage). *Fun:* a tutorial; "can't tell" is correct 6 times out of 8, so the quiz teaches one trick. *Visual:* the allele-on-each-wing-case beetle is a genuinely good icon; the field is a static grid on a beige gradient. *Redesign:* fold into a 60-second opener: a breeder orders "three yellow beetles", and the player must find hidden carriers among blue beetles by test-crossing against aa. The player learns "phenotype hides genotype" by needing it.

**L2 Break into alleles.** *Does:* guesses a count, presses a button, watches tokens fly to jars; 3 rounds. *Science:* correct, and "31 carriers but A is the minority allele" is a good line. *Fun:* the jar animation is satisfying the first time and then just a delay. *Visual:* best animation in the game. *Redesign:* make it an estimation game. Swarms of 100–400 moving beetles, a 5-second look, estimate p, and score by error. Later rounds allow phenotype only, so the player *must* assume HW (q = √aa) to estimate. That sets up L16 and plants the doubt "what if HW doesn't hold?"

**L3 Dominant isn't common.** *Does:* one MCQ, one slider guess; 90 s. *Science:* correct. The Yule–Punnett–Hardy history (lv_a.js:122) is right in outline: Yule (1902) argued a dominant should rise to 3:1, and Punnett took the problem to Hardy (Edwards 2008, *Genetics* 179:1143). *Fun:* a single twist, not a level. *Redesign:* make the history playable. The player is Yule, predicts brachydactyly will reach 75%, runs 50 generations, sees nothing happen, and Hardy's letter appears. Merge it with L2.

**L4 The gamete pool.** *Does:* presses Draw 1/10/100 until 100 offspring exist. *Science:* correct (draws with replacement from 40 tokens). *Fun:* clicker with no decision. *Visual:* tokens flying to egg/sperm slots work; the nest of 120 tiny beetles is unreadable. *Redesign:* see L4–L6 below.

**L5 Why p²?** *Does:* sets a slider to hit 4 targets. *Science:* correct. *Fun:* sliding until a number matches is guess-and-check, not insight; three misses reveal the answer. *Visual:* the area square is the right idea, drawn plainly.

**L6 Why 2pq?** *Does:* taps 4 cells, one MCQ, watches tiles collapse into a bar, picks 1 of 4 bars. *Science:* correct, and the distractor that "forgot the second route" (79%) is excellent. *Visual:* the tile collapse is the best "equation appears from a picture" moment in the repo.

*Redesign for L4–L6 together ("The Mixer", one level, about 6 min):* a physical lottery. Eggs fall from a left hopper whose A share is p_f; sperm come from a right hopper with share p_m. Each pair lands in a 2×2 bin whose column and row widths are the hopper shares, so p² literally fills a square. Escalation: (1) p_f = p_m: the player predicts bar heights before releasing 500 pairs. (2) The **twist**: p_f = 0.9, p_m = 0.3. Offspring are not in p²/2pq/q² proportions (AA = 0.27, not 0.36), but one more round of mating puts them there. This teaches the hidden assumption "equal allele frequencies in the sexes" and sets up X-linkage, where equality is never reached in one step. (3) Boss: the hoppers are hidden; infer p_f and p_m from offspring counts.

**L7 One generation.** *Does:* taps a predicted point on the triangle, 3 populations. *Science:* correct for an autosomal locus with equal sex frequencies. "Exactly p², 2pq, q²" is deterministic rounding (`hwCounts`). In a real population of 40 there would be sampling noise, and L4 had just shown noise. *Fun:* the one level where prediction is spatial and checked against a map. It works. *Redesign:* keep it. Add a fourth population that is X-linked. It spirals in and does not jump, and that boss shows the "one generation" rule has conditions.

**L8 Equilibrium Island.** *Does:* toggles five switches, runs 12 generations, then 4 mystery islands with 5 buttons each. *Science:* the migration text is wrong (above). The mutation rate is 3% per generation, a fantasy rate, and mutation is excluded from the mysteries. Viability selection (`w = [1,1,0.55]`) applied after mating puts *adults* slightly off the curve, so "slides along the curve" (lv_b.js:123) is approximate. The double negatives ("✗ No selection") are confusing. *Fun:* the mysteries are the most game-like moment in Equilibrium, but each force has one fixed, exaggerated magnitude and one direction, so after one round the player matches patterns ("went right = migration"). *Visual:* the triangle trail is clear; the beetle panel adds nothing. *Redesign ("The Saboteur"):* the computer secretly breaks 1–3 assumptions at realistic, randomised strengths and directions. The player has a budget of field days to spend on instruments: genotype a sample of n (cost ∝ n), split by region, split by age class, count arrivals at the harbour, mark–recapture. Score = correct diagnosis × budget left. Escalation goes from single forces to opposed pairs. Boss: **a population where p never moves but adults sit above the curve.** That is heterozygote advantage at equilibrium: "not evolving" and "selection is acting" are both true. This level should absorb L15 and the Case Files.

**L9 The bird.** *Does:* taps up to 10 static beetles per generation for up to 3 generations, then watches a 20-generation curve. *Science:* q_t = q₀/(1 + tq₀) for complete selection is correct (lv_c.js:24), and "share of a copies in heterozygotes = p" is correct. The general formula Δq = −spq²/(1 − sq²) is never shown, and selection against a *dominant* allele is never contrasted. *Fun:* the beetles do not move, the ground is a flat gradient, and yellow is trivially visible ("Yellow beetles stand out on this ground" when there is no ground). The lens cheat breaks it. *Visual:* the weakest predator level in the repo, sitting next to Tangled Bank's superb Lichen Wood engine. *Redesign:* reuse the Tangled Bank / Endless Forms hunt engine (textured background, timed mornings, miss penalty). Run it twice: once against a recessive yellow, once against a dominant yellow. The player *feels* that the dominant allele goes in a few generations while the recessive tail lingers. Boss: the Black Country with real-style numbers. The carbonaria rise is fast and then slows as typica hides in heterozygotes, and Haldane (1924) used exactly this curve to estimate s ≈ 0.33.

**L10 The slow ray.** *Does:* one MCQ, two "run" buttons, one MCQ. *Science:* p_t = p₀(1 − μ)^t is correct; 10⁻⁵ per locus per generation is a fair gene-level rate. Back mutation and the equilibrium p̂ = ν/(μ + ν) are missing. The central applied idea, **mutation–selection balance** (q̂ ≈ √(μ/s) for recessives, ≈ μ/s for dominants), is absent from the whole game. *Fun:* the most boring level here: a number changes in the third decimal place. *Redesign ("The Leaky Tap"):* mutation is a tap dripping a-alleles into the pool, and selection is a drain whose size depends on how many aa are visible. The player adjusts s and μ to hold q at a target. The twist: making the allele recessive multiplies its equilibrium frequency by orders of magnitude (√(μ/s) vs μ/s). For μ = 10⁻⁵ and s = 1, that is q ≈ 0.003 against 0.00001. That explains why recessive diseases persist and connects straight to L16.

**L11 The bridge.** *Does:* one MCQ, then a migration slider and "run a season". *Science:* p′ = (1 − m)p + m·p_m is correct, and 0.375 is right. Symmetric exchange converging to 0.55 is correct for equal island sizes, which the game never states. *Fun:* no tension, because nothing opposes migration. *Visual:* beetles sliding between two green blobs. *Redesign ("Gene Swamping"):* each island has local selection (dark on lava, pale on sand), and the player builds and removes ferries on a budget. Too little migration and drift erodes small islands; too much and local adaptation is swamped (migration–selection balance: adaptation survives roughly while m < s). Boss: keep every island polymorphic *and* locally adapted for 100 generations, which brings out the "one migrant per generation" rule of thumb (Nm ≈ 1).

**L12 Five tiny islands.** *Does:* one MCQ, three size buttons, one slider challenge. *Science:* Wright–Fisher binomial sampling and "lose 1/2N of heterozygosity per generation" are correct. It uses census N throughout, and **Ne is never introduced**. *Fun:* the spreading lines are the best visual in Equilibrium. The "smallest size" challenge does not reward small (N = 200 passes), and it is pure luck. *Redesign ("Buri's Bottles"):* 100 bottles of 16 flies (Buri 1956 really did this). The histogram of p spreads into a U-shape, and the player places bets on how it will look. Then come Ne puzzles: same N but a 9:1 sex ratio (Ne = 4NmNf/(Nm+Nf) = 14.4 from 40), a population that crashes every fifth year (harmonic mean), and equalised family sizes in captive breeding (Ne ≈ 2N). Boss: a zoo manager must keep 90% heterozygosity for 50 generations on a fixed enclosure budget.

**L13 Volcano and castaways.** *Does:* one MCQ, watch, tap 5 beetles, watch. *Science:* expected H decay is fine. Using one biallelic locus at p = 0.5 is the worst possible case for the real lesson: a bottleneck of 4 keeps about 87.5% of H in that generation and almost never loses an allele. Bottlenecks mainly strip **allelic richness** (rare alleles) and remove relatively little heterozygosity if recovery is fast (Nei, Maruyama & Chakraborty 1975). The castaway choice has no consequence: `const ok = true; this.score++` (lv_c.js:123). *Fun:* a cutscene. *Redesign:* use a 12-allele locus drawn as 12 colours. The eruption leaves survivors, and the twist is that the heterozygosity bar barely drops while half the colours vanish forever. The player then chooses founders under phenotype-only vision for a reintroduction and is scored on alleles retained. Real anchors: northern elephant seals, cheetahs. Merge with Endless Forms' *The Castaways*, which covers founders.

**L14 The matchmaker.** *Does:* pairs 6 same-colour beetles by tapping, then presses 3 mating-rule buttons. *Science:* the disassortative error is above. It also treats inbreeding and assortative mating as the same thing. Inbreeding raises homozygosity at *every* locus, assortative mating only at loci affecting the trait. **F is never named**, even though heterozygosity is 2pq(1 − F) and the aa share q² + pqF are the whole point. *Fun:* the pairing step is busywork with a forced rule. *Redesign ("The Pedigree"):* build F by path-counting through a family tree. The visual hook is recessive conditions surfacing in cousin-marriage generations. Boss: the Spanish Habsburgs, where Charles II's F ≈ 0.25 (Alvarez, Ceballos & Quinteiro 2009, *PLoS ONE*). Then show that assortative mating for colour leaves an unlinked locus on the curve while inbreeding drops both.

**L15 Evolving, or just unbalanced?** *Does:* watches 4 trails, picks one of 4 labels. *Science:* the distinction is the right one to teach. *Fun:* 4 cases and 4 labels used once each, so the last one is free. *Redesign:* absorb it into The Saboteur (L8).

**L16 Invisible carriers.** *Does:* 2 MCQs, a slider target, one CF MCQ. *Science:* correct (CF about 1 in 2,500, carriers about 1 in 25; 198 carriers per affected individual at q = 0.01). It should add that CFTR has more than 2,000 variants, so "the a allele" is a lumped class, and that consanguinity or population structure inflates aa, which biases q = √aa upward. *Fun:* the 50-to-1 carrier crowd is a strong visual. *Redesign:* make the player a genetic counsellor. A couple from a q = 0.02 population: risk 1/25 × 1/25 × 1/4 = 1/2,500. They are first cousins: recompute with F = 1/16. The boss is a population with hidden structure, where the HW-based estimate is wrong and the player must spot it.

**L17 Real populations.** *Does:* reads counts, presses "work it out", picks fits/departs, 3 times. *Science:* χ² with 1 df and the 3.84 threshold are correct (3 classes − 1 − 1 estimated parameter). The scarlet tiger moth caveat ("a good fit does not prove nothing is happening") is excellent, and Sheppard (1952) did report non-random mating there. The sickle-cell data are the classic adult survey, and its heterozygote excess and SS deficit are correct (Allison 1954). Problems: a level titled "Real populations" includes a fictional third case; two samples have N > 17,000, so the student never meets low **power**. HW tests detect only very strong viability selection (Lewontin & Cockerham 1959). Codominant data (e.g. MN blood groups) would let the player count genotypes directly. *Redesign:* merge with Endless Forms' *The Census* sampling-with-cost mechanic. The player chooses n. With n = 40 even F = 0.2 is often "not significant", and with n = 30,000 trivial deviations are. Boss: sickle-cell newborns vs adults.

**Case Files.** *Does:* 8 cases, 10 MCQs; about 5 min. *Science:* mostly sound (the Wahlund case is consistent with p₁ = 0.9, p₂ = 0.1), apart from the two inconsistencies above. *Fun:* these are reading-comprehension tests. Each prompt states its answer ("Mark–recapture: 70% of blue survive…", "a ferry service began", "No selection has been detected"), and in the final case the correct option is the only long one. The design brief (doc 04) asked for evidence that has to be disentangled, and this does not deliver it. *Redesign:* each case opens with only a p-trace. The player buys evidence (genotype sample, regional split, age split, migrant count, survival study) from a limited budget, commits to a diagnosis with a confidence level, and is scored on calibration. Boss: a case where HW fits perfectly but selection is acting.

## Tangled Bank, chapter by chapter

**I The Lichen Wood (hunt).** *Does:* 11-second mornings, tap up to 15 moths, about 10–16 generations of polygenic crypsis. *Science:* Bond & Kamil (2002, *Nature*) is the right citation; heritability "roughly" is honest. *Fun:* it works because the verb (search and tap) is a real skill and evolution makes the player's job harder. That is the core loop the whole project should copy. *Visual:* procedural lichen bark with moth sprites, excellent. *Redesign:* already ported to Endless Forms. Add apostatic selection (Bond & Kamil's polymorphism): the player's search image should *create* a spread of morphs, and the chart should show it.

**II The Black Country (hunt).** *Does:* the same verb while soot arrives, then the Clean Air Act. *Science:* history correct (1848, about 98% by 1895, van't Hof 2016, Cook et al. 2012), but the mu = 0.01 problem stands. It misses the HW payoff: carbonaria is dominant, so its rise slows as typica becomes a hidden recessive, and its post-1960 decline is fast. *Redesign:* overlay the Gene Pool lens (phenotype / genotype / allele jars) and show the carrier tail. This is where Equilibrium L9 should live.

**III Bitter Wings (hunt).** *Does:* eat palewings, retch on bitterwings (−3 s). *Science:* "mimics only work while they are rare" is too strong. Batesian mimicry is negative frequency-dependent and works *best* when rare. *Fun/visual:* strong. *Redesign:* add a Müllerian level (two toxic species converge) and a mimic-frequency slider, so the player's learning visibly breaks down as mimics become common.

**IV Streams of Aripo (steward).** *Does:* one transplant button and two predator toggles, then waits up to 160 generations. *Science:* Endler (1980), Reznick life-history follow-ups and Rodd et al. (2002) sensory bias are all correct; the "offspring vs females" wording is the fault noted above. *Fun:* three decisions in ten minutes. *Visual:* lovely pools, but the guppies are specks. *Redesign:* the player is a female guppy choosing among males for a few rounds, *then* zooms out to the steward view to see the population consequence. Two scales, one session.

**V The Slow Island (steward).** *Does:* found a colony, toggle foxes, wait up to 1,500 years. *Science:* Austad (1993); the Medawar/Williams logic and the Y-model trade-off plus mutation accumulation are all correct and well implemented. *Fun:* mostly watching. "Grey fur marks the old" is a lovely touch. *Redesign:* show life tables as stacked age bars the player can compare; add a decision such as choosing which age class a hazard hits.

**VI The Commons (steward).** *Does:* rain slider, founders slider, flood button. *Science:* the haystack model and the Price decomposition (between = Cov(w, x)/w̄, within = E(wΔx)/w̄) are implemented correctly. The live between/within bars are the single best piece of conceptual visualisation in this slice. *Fun:* this is the steward chapter that works, because timing floods is a real decision. *Redesign:* already in Endless Forms. Keep the Price bars as a reusable HUD widget.

**What to salvage from Tangled Bank.** (1) The `Gen` engine (diploid, additive, dominant and mutation-count loci, free recombination) as the *single* genetics engine of the combined game. Equilibrium's count-based `wfNext/hwCounts` is a special case of it. (2) The art direction (dark naturalist palette, procedural textures). (3) **Steward mode as the capstone sandbox, "The Tangled Bank":** a map of linked habitats (islands, streams, woods) where the player places predators, bridges, soot, floods and captive-breeding pens. Any population can be opened with the Gene Pool lens and its de Finetti dot. Scenario cards set the goals ("make carbonaria rise and fall", "keep 3 alleles on 5 islands for 200 generations with 2 bridges", "evolve slow ageing in under 500 years"). This is where all five HW forces meet ecology, and where a professor can set open problems.

## Missing concepts in this area

- **Heterozygote advantage and balanced polymorphism.** *"Malaria Coast":* the player sets malaria intensity across a coastline. HbS rises to q̂ = s/(s + t) where malaria is present and falls where it is absent. Newborns sit on the HW curve and adults sit above it, which the triangle shows. Boss: eliminate malaria and watch how slowly the S allele declines.
- **Mutation–selection balance.** *"The Leaky Tap"* (above): the dominant vs recessive equilibrium gap is the payoff.
- **Multiple alleles.** *"Blood Bank":* ABO with three alleles. The square becomes 3×3 and (p + q + r)² builds a hexagonal tiling; the player matches donors, estimating r from type-O frequency (r = √O). It also sets up allelic richness for the bottleneck level.
- **X-linkage.** *"The Royal Line":* males show q and females q². Allele frequencies in the two sexes oscillate toward equality, which the triangle shows as a spiral, not a jump. Queen Victoria's haemophilia pedigree is the boss.
- **Linkage disequilibrium and its decay.** *"Tangled Pairs":* two loci and four gamete types; D decays by (1 − r) per generation, faster for loose linkage. Hitchhiking follows when a sweep drags a neutral allele with it, which connects to Endless Forms' *The Sweep*.
- **Population structure and F-statistics.** *"Valley Survey":* sample several demes; F_ST = Var(p)/p̄q̄; the pooled heterozygote deficit equals 2Var(p) (Wahlund). The player places sampling sites to find a hidden barrier.
- **Effective population size.** Sex ratio, fluctuating size and family-size variance (Buri's Bottles, above). Ne/N is often about 0.1 in wild populations (Frankham 1995).
- **Selection and drift together: Haldane's sieve and the Ns rule.** *"The Lottery of the New":* release 100 copies of a new beneficial mutation with s = 0.02. About 96 are lost to drift (fixation probability ≈ 2s), and dominant beneficial mutations establish more often than recessive ones (Haldane's sieve). Then vary N: when Ns < 1, selection is invisible, which is the nearly neutral theory.
- **Frequency-dependent mating systems.** Self-incompatibility alleles: a rare S allele can mate with everyone, so S-alleles multiply to dozens. This is the honest replacement for the false disassortative-mating claim.

## Cross-cutting observations

1. **Interaction grammar.** Equilibrium spends about 80% of its time on panel text plus MCQ. Stars reward quiz answers, not mastery of a system. Rule for the combined game: no star should ever come from a multiple-choice click. Prediction is good, but it should be spatial (tap the triangle, sketch the curve) and scored on distance.
2. **Deterministic and stochastic models are mixed without saying so.** `hwCounts` gives perfect proportions in L7, L9, L11 and L13 visuals, but noisy draws in L4 and L12. Undergraduates need both, side by side: "expected" as a ghost and "realised" as the solid.
3. **Exaggerated parameters with no labels.** Mutation at 3% per generation (L8), m = 0.15, a recurrent carbonaria rate of 1%. Every non-realistic parameter should carry a visible "×1000 speed-up" tag; L10 already does this well.
4. **Two Hardy–Weinberg courses.** Equilibrium uses dominant blue/yellow beetles. Endless Forms' *The Census* (ch13.js) uses codominant snails and better mechanics: sampling costs, strata, and a correct p-value (`2(1 − Φ(√χ²))`). Its maths text correctly gives the Wahlund deficit as 2 × variance and defines F. Merge them: the derivation (Equilibrium L1–L7 compressed to about 12 minutes), then The Saboteur / Census detective, all with one organism, one colour code and one allele notation.
5. **Make HW a lens, not a chapter.** The most valuable thing Equilibrium invented is the trio of phenotype lens, genotype lens, allele jars, plus the triangle mini-map. Put it in the HUD of every population chapter (Lichen Wood, Black Country, Drift Casino, Castaways, Clinic, Islands). Then HW is the instrument panel the student uses all game, not a course they take once.
6. **Visuals.** Equilibrium's static beige field ("a quiet field, nothing moves", engine.js:124) is deliberate austerity that reads as unfinished next to Tangled Bank. Beetles in grids never walk, and phenotype never interacts with a background. Adopt Tangled Bank's art direction everywhere. Tangled Bank itself renders a phone-width column even on a 1280-px desktop, wasting two-thirds of the screen.
7. **Assumptions never stated.** Diploidy, sexual reproduction, non-overlapping generations, an autosomal locus and equal allele frequencies in the sexes are all used and never named. Two of the proposed twists (unequal sexes, X-linkage) make the student find them.
8. **Technology (brief).** These are 2D agent simulations with charts. Unity's WebGL builds are heavy and slow to load on school Chromebooks. Recommended stack: one TypeScript codebase, simulation modules kept separate from rendering (pure and unit-testable, so the science can be audited), PixiJS/WebGL for thousands of animated organisms, and Capacitor or Tauri wrappers for app stores. Godot 4 is the credible alternative if a native-app feel matters more than web reach. Use Blender only to pre-render sprites. For teachers the bigger needs are not graphics: class codes, a teacher dashboard of misconceptions (which wrong answers, which predictions), and a sandbox scenario editor.
